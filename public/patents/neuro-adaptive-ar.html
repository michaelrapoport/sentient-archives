Neuro-Adaptive Augmented Reality Target Acquisition and Correction System

ABSTRACT

[0001] A neuro-adaptive augmented reality (AR) target correction system integrates a high-fidelity brain-computer interface (BCI) with a dynamic AR display. The system captures neural oscillations, specifically identifying phase-amplitude coupling (PAC) between mid-frontal theta (4-8 Hz) and high-gamma (70-110 Hz) bands to decode subconscious target registration. A neural spatial-probability field is generated via source localization (e.g., sLORETA) and fused with kinematic state-vectors of a dynamic object using an Unscented Kalman Filter (UKF). When a subconscious discrepancy is detected, the system executes a trajectory warp within the AR display. The warping is governed by a temporal lead-time ($t_{lead}$) inversely proportional to parietal alpha-band power, and is scaled to suppress the formation of error-related negativity (ERN) signals, rendering the correction neuro-transparent to the user.

FIELD OF THE INVENTION

[0002] The present invention relates generally to the field of augmented reality (AR) systems, and more particularly to AR systems that utilize brain-computer interfaces (BCIs) to enhance target acquisition and correction through the integration of subconscious neural feedback and kinematic predictive modeling.

BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

[0003] Existing AR systems for target acquisition typically rely on computer vision algorithms, sensor fusion, and predictive modeling to enhance a user's ability to identify and engage targets. These systems often overlay information onto the user's field of view, such as target range, bearing, and predicted trajectory. However, these conventional approaches are limited by the processing speed of the computer vision algorithms, the accuracy of the sensor data, and the inherent cognitive limitations of the user. Furthermore, these systems fail to address the subconscious cognitive processes that contribute to aiming errors.
[0004] Brain-computer interfaces (BCIs) have been used in various applications, including assistive technologies and gaming. While BCIs have shown promise in controlling external devices and providing feedback to users, their application in AR target acquisition systems has been limited. Existing BCI-controlled AR systems often require extensive user training and are prone to errors and inconsistencies. Most importantly, current systems lack the ability to intervene in a manner that is "neuro-transparent"—that is, correcting for errors before the user becomes consciously aware of them, thereby avoiding the elicitation of error-related negativity (ERN) which can disrupt user performance.

SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

[0005] The present invention provides a neuro-adaptive augmented reality (AR) target correction system that detects subconscious error signals when a user, such as a operator, pilot, or marksman, is aiming at a target. The system automatically corrects the perceived target trajectory in the user's field of view, thereby improving accuracy and performance.
[0006] The system comprises a high-fidelity BCI configured to record neural oscillations from a user's cortical topography, an AR display system configured to render a dynamic object trajectory, and a processor. The processor is configured to analyze neural oscillations to identify phase-amplitude coupling (PAC), generate a neural spatial-probability field, and integrate said field with a kinematic state-vector via an Unscented Kalman Filter (UKF).
[0007] A key aspect of the invention is the closed-loop trajectory warping which is scaled by the confidence interval of the UKF. This warping is timed according to the user's instantaneous alpha-band power to ensure the manipulation remains subconscious. By suppressing the formation of subsequent ERN signals, the system maintains high performance without increasing cognitive load or causing the "uncanny valley" effect in motion perception.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

[0011] Reference will now be made in detail to the present preferred embodiments of the invention, examples of which are illustrated in the accompanying drawings. The system 100 generally comprises a Neuro-Sensing Layer 110, a Kinematic Layer 120, a Core Processor 130, and an AR Display 140.
[0012] The Neuro-Sensing Layer 110 utilizes a 64-channel dry-electrode EEG cap 111. The Pre-processor 112 implements Artifact Subspace Reconstruction (ASR) to eliminate myogenic noise. The system specifically targets Phase-Amplitude Coupling (PAC) 113. The relationship between the phase of the mid-frontal theta ($\theta$) and the amplitude of the high-gamma ($\gamma$) is analyzed using a Modulation Index (MI) defined as:
\[ MI = \frac{D_{KL}(P, U)}{\log(N)} \]
Where $D_{KL}(P, U)$ is the Kullback-Leibler divergence between the empirical phase-amplitude distribution $P$ and a uniform distribution $U$, and $N$ is the number of phase bins.
[0013] Upon detection of a subconscious target registration via PAC, Source Localization 114 is performed using sLORETA (standardized low-resolution brain electromagnetic tomography). This maps the gamma burst to the Anterior Cingulate Cortex (ACC) and the Pre-supplementary Motor Area (pre-SMA) to generate the Neural Spatial-Probability Field 115, which serves as an estimated intention coordinate in 3D space.
[0014] The Kinematic Layer 120 utilizes LiDAR or high-speed computer vision 121 to track a dynamic object, generating a state-vector 122 comprised of position ($x$), velocity ($v$), and acceleration ($a$). This state-vector is fused with the Neural Spatial-Probability Field 115 in an Unscented Kalman Filter (UKF) 131.
[0015] The UKF 131 addresses the non-linearities of the ballistic or guided flight path. When the Discrepancy Detection 133 identifies a significant variance between the user's intent and the object's path, the Trajectory Warping 134 module calculates a temporal lead-time ($t_{lead}$) to initiate the visual shift. This lead-time is critical for neuro-transparency and is determined by:
\[ t_{lead} = \frac{k}{P_{\alpha}} \]
Where $P_{\alpha}$ is the instantaneous power of the parietal alpha-band (8-12 Hz) and $k$ is a calibration constant. High alpha power indicates lower cortical excitability, requiring a longer lead-time for the warping to be perceived as a natural motion of the object.
[0016] The AR Display 140 executes the warp using a Vertex Shader 141. To prevent the user from consciously detecting the manipulation, a Gaussian blur ($\sigma = 2.5$) is applied to the peripheral pixels of the object, while the focal point 141c remains sharp. If an ERN signal—a negative deflection at the FCz electrode occurring approximately 50-100ms after a perceived error—is detected, the system adaptively reduces the UKF confidence interval, effectively smoothing the correction in subsequent iterations.

CLAIMS

1. A neuro-adaptive augmented reality (AR) target correction system, the system comprising:
a high-fidelity brain-computer interface (BCI) configured to record neural oscillations from a user's cortical topography;
an AR display system configured to render a dynamic object trajectory; and
a processor configured to:
    analyze the neural oscillations to identify a phase-amplitude coupling (PAC) between a phase of a mid-frontal theta-band (4-8 Hz) oscillation and an amplitude of a high-gamma band (70-110 Hz) burst occurring during a pre-impact phase of the dynamic object trajectory;
    generate a neural spatial-probability field by applying a source localization algorithm to decode the high-gamma band burst;
    integrate the neural spatial-probability field with a kinematic state-vector of the dynamic object via an Unscented Kalman Filter (UKF) to generate a predicted future state-space intersection with a target, the prediction having an associated confidence interval;
    detect a subconscious discrepancy between the predicted future state-space intersection and the dynamic object trajectory; and
    execute a closed-loop trajectory warping of the dynamic object within the AR display system to align the dynamic object trajectory with the predicted future state-space intersection at a temporal lead-time ($t_{lead}$), wherein the processor calculates $t_{lead}$ according to an inverse function of the user's instantaneous alpha-band (8-12 Hz) power ($P_{alpha}$) such that $t_{lead} = k / P_{alpha}$, where k is a pre-calibrated constant, and wherein a magnitude of the trajectory warping is scaled by the confidence interval of the UKF to suppress a subsequent formation of an error-related negativity (ERN) signal.
2. The system of claim 1, wherein the trajectory warping comprises a non-linear spatial transformation that applies a Gaussian blur to a peripheral region of the dynamic object while maintaining a high-contrast focal point aligned with a centroid of the predicted future state-space intersection.
3. The system of claim 1, wherein the processor is further configured to adaptively update the constant $k$ based on a baseline measurement of the user's ERN amplitude during a calibration phase, thereby normalizing the lead-time adjustment to individual neuro-physiological response latencies.
4. The system of claim 1, further comprising an eye-tracking sensor, wherein the processor is configured to gate the integration of the neural spatial-probability field with the kinematic state-vector only when the user's gaze vector is within a threshold angular distance of the dynamic object.
5. The system of claim 1, wherein the source localization algorithm is selected from the group consisting of standardized low-resolution brain electromagnetic tomography (sLORETA) and linearly constrained minimum variance (LCMV) beamforming.
6. The system of claim 1, wherein the high-fidelity BCI comprises a multi-channel electroencephalography (EEG) system utilizing at least 64 dry electrodes configured according to the International 10-20 system.
7. The system of claim 1, wherein the AR display system comprises a head-mounted display (HMD) and the processor is implemented in C++ within a real-time Linux kernel with RT-Preempt patches to maintain a total system latency of less than 20 milliseconds.
