PHONON-COUPLED MEMRISTIVE LOGIC ARCHITECTURE UTILIZING SHARED THERMAL SUBSTRATES FOR NON-ELECTRICAL INTERCONNECTION

Inventor: Michael Rapoport from Polaritronics Inc.

ABSTRACT

[0001] A logic gate architecture is provided that utilizes a shared thermal medium for signal interconnection, effectively reducing electrical wiring overhead. The architecture comprises a plurality of memristive devices disposed in close spatial proximity on a single, suspended, and thermally-isolated island structure. Upon a state change in a primary memristor, generated Joule heat propagates as phonons across the shared island. This localized thermal flux modulates the switching threshold of adjacent memristive devices due to their intrinsic thermal coefficients. The resulting thermal coupling enables the implementation of Boolean logic functions, including lateral inhibition and coincidence detection, mediated by physical phonon propagation through the shared substrate rather than direct electrical conductive paths.

FIELD OF THE INVENTION

[0002] The present invention relates generally to semiconductor logic architectures and memristive devices. More particularly, the invention relates to non-electrical interconnects and computational structures that utilize thermal propagation (phonons) within a shared substrate to perform logical operations.

BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

[0003] Conventional integrated circuits rely on metallic interconnects to facilitate communication between logic gates. As device dimensions scale toward the sub-10nm regime, the area, power consumption, and signal latency associated with these electrical interconnects have become primary bottlenecks in computational efficiency, a phenomenon often termed the "interconnect tyranny."

[0004] While memristive devices offer promising pathways for non-volatile storage and neuromorphic computing, their integration into complex logic circuits typically continues to rely on traditional electrical wiring for signal routing. There exists a significant need for alternative interconnect modalities that can leverage the intrinsic physical properties of the substrate to perform local information transfer and computation without the parasitic overhead of metal lines.

SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

[0005] The present invention addresses the aforementioned challenges by providing a logic gate architecture constructed upon a shared, thermally-conductive island. This island is thermally decoupled from the surrounding bulk substrate via isolation means, such as suspension or dielectric trenches.

[0006] In one embodiment, the architecture comprises at least two memristive devices fabricated on the shared island. The first device (Input A) functions as a heat source upon activation, while the second device (Input B) functions as a thermally-sensitive receiver. The propagation of phonons through the island alters the local temperature ($T$) of the second device, thereby modifying its switching threshold voltage ($V_{th}$).

[0007] By engineering the thermal resistance of the island and the thermal coefficient of the memristors, the system can implement inhibitory logic. Specifically, if Input A is triggered, the resulting heat pulse increases the $V_{th}$ of Input B, preventing it from switching even if an electrical input is simultaneously applied. This provides a physical mechanism for lateral inhibition and complex logic gates without direct electrical wiring between the inputs of the devices.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

[0011] Referring to FIG. 1 and FIG. 2, the present invention comprises a Shared Thermal Island (100) fabricated from a material with high thermal conductivity, such as Silicon Nitride ($Si_3N_4$) or Diamond-like Carbon (DLC). The island (100) is physically separated from the Bulk Substrate (105) by a Thermal Isolation Trench (130), which may comprise an air gap, vacuum, or a low-thermal-conductivity dielectric (e.g., Silicon Dioxide).

[0012] Disposed on the island (100) are a plurality of memristive devices, including Memristor A (110) and Memristor B (120). Each memristor comprises a switching layer (e.g., $TiO_x$, $HfO_x$, or $TaO_x$) situated between conductive electrodes. The electrical switching behavior of these devices is intrinsically linked to their local temperature.

[0013] The mathematical relationship governing the switching threshold voltage ($V_{th}$) as a function of temperature ($T$) is defined as:

$$V_{th}(T) = V_{th,0} + \alpha(T - T_{ref})$$

Where $V_{th,0}$ is the threshold voltage at a reference temperature $T_{ref}$, and $\alpha$ is the thermal coefficient of switching. In the preferred embodiment, $\alpha > 0$, indicating that the voltage required to trigger a state change increases as the island (100) heats up.

[0014] When an electrical signal is applied to Memristor A (110), it undergoes a SET or RESET operation, generating a quantity of Joule heat ($Q$). This heat propagates via Phonon Flux (140) across the island (100). The temperature rise at Memristor B (120) following a time delay ($\Delta t$) is characterized by the thermal diffusivity ($D$) of the island material:

$$\Delta T(r, t) = \frac{Q}{(4\pi Dt)^{3/2}} \exp\left(-\frac{r^2}{4Dt}\right)$$

Where $r$ is the spatial distance between the memristors. By configuring the distance $r$ and the diffusivity $D$, the logic gate is "timed" to inhibit Device B (120) precisely when Device A (110) is active.

[0015] This architecture enables a "Lateral Inhibition" logic gate. In this configuration, Memristor A (110) acts as an inhibitory input. If A is "1" (active), the local environment of B (120) becomes thermally biased, such that the input signal required to switch B to "1" exceeds the available pulse magnitude. Thus, the state of B depends on the thermal history of the shared island (100).

CLAIMS

  • A logic gate apparatus, comprising:
    • a thermally-isolated island (100) characterized by high internal thermal conductivity; and
    • a plurality of memristive devices (110, 120) disposed on said thermally-isolated island, each of said devices having a temperature-dependent switching characteristic;
    • wherein the plurality of memristive devices are configured such that Joule heat generated by a first memristive device (110) propagates through the thermally-isolated island (100) to modulate the temperature of at least a second memristive device (120), thereby modifying the switching characteristic of the second memristive device.
  • The apparatus of claim 1, wherein the modulation of the switching characteristic implements a Boolean logic function without a direct electrical interconnect between the first and second memristive devices.
  • The apparatus of claim 2, wherein the Boolean logic function is lateral inhibition, characterized by the activity of the first memristive device (110) increasing the switching threshold of the second memristive device (120).
  • The apparatus of claim 1, wherein the thermally-isolated island (100) comprises a suspended bridge structure separated from a substrate (105) by an air gap or vacuum.
  • The apparatus of claim 1, wherein the thermally-isolated island (100) is defined by a dielectric trench (130) having a thermal conductivity lower than that of the island.
  • The apparatus of claim 1, wherein the communication between the first and second memristive devices is mediated exclusively by phonon propagation through the thermally-isolated island (100).
  • The apparatus of claim 1, wherein the memristive devices (110, 120) comprise a transition metal oxide switching layer.
  • A method for performing a logic operation in a solid-state device, the method comprising:
    • providing a plurality of memristors on a shared, thermally-conductive, and isolated island;
    • applying a first electrical pulse to a first memristor to generate a phonon flux within the island;
    • propagating the phonon flux to a second memristor to raise a local temperature of the second memristor; and
    • modulating a threshold switching voltage of the second memristor via the raised local temperature to inhibit or enable a state change in response to a second electrical pulse.
