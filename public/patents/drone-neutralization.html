Non-Kinetic Drone Neutralization System Utilizing Stimulated Raman Adiabatic Passage

ABSTRACT

[0001]
A system and method for the non-kinetic neutralization of unmanned aerial systems (UAS) utilizes a spectrographic sensor to identify molecular resonances within a target material. A multi-channel laser assembly, comprising a pump laser and a Stokes laser, is controlled by a processor configured to execute a Stimulated Raman Adiabatic Passage (STIRAP) protocol. By delivering a counter-intuitive pulse sequence—where the Stokes pulse precedes the pump pulse in a spatiotemporally overlapped region—the system induces a coherent population transfer from a molecular bonding ground state to a repulsive electronic state. This process bypasses intermediate thermalization, leading to the rapid structural dissociation of the target material's molecular matrix, such as the epoxy resins in carbon-fiber-reinforced polymers, effectively neutralizing the UAS without kinetic impact.

FIELD OF THE INVENTION

[0002]
The present invention relates generally to countermeasures for unmanned aerial systems (UAS). More specifically, the invention relates to a non-kinetic system that employs spectroscopic identification and directed energy via Stimulated Raman Adiabatic Passage (STIRAP) to destabilize the structural integrity of UAS materials at the molecular level.

BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

[0003]
The proliferation of unmanned aerial systems (UAS), or drones, has introduced significant security challenges. Conventional countermeasures generally fall into two categories: kinetic and electronic. Kinetic countermeasures, such as projectiles, nets, or high-energy thermal lasers, rely on physical impact or localized heating. These methods often result in collateral damage from falling debris or secondary fires.
[0004]
Electronic countermeasures, such as signal jamming or protocol manipulation, attempt to sever the command-and-control link of the UAS. However, modern autonomous drones utilizing inertial navigation and pre-programmed mission logic are increasingly immune to link-disruption techniques.
[0005]
There exists a critical need for a counter-UAS system that is non-kinetic (to minimize collateral damage) yet physical in its effect (to bypass electronic hardening). Prior art lacks the ability to selectively and coherently manipulate the molecular bonds of UAS structural components to induce "cold" dissociation without the requirement for massive thermal energy transfer or physical impact.

SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

[0006]
The present invention provides a system for non-kinetic drone neutralization comprising a spectrographic sensor, a multi-channel laser assembly, and a controller. The system identifies the specific polymer or composite resin used in a UAS airframe and applies a quantum-mechanical protocol to dissociate the molecular bonds holding the material together.
[0007]
Specifically, the system utilizes Stimulated Raman Adiabatic Passage (STIRAP) to induce a coherent population transfer from a bonding ground state ($|1\rangle$) to a repulsive electronic state ($|3\rangle$) via a virtual or intermediate state ($|2\rangle$). This is achieved by a "counter-intuitive" timing of pulses where the Stokes beam is applied slightly before the pump beam, creating a "dark state" polariton that prevents the energy from being lost to heat (vibrational thermalization), instead channeling it directly into bond dissociation.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

[0012]
Referring to FIG. 1, the system 100 comprises a spectrographic sensor 102, which in the preferred embodiment is a Coherent Anti-Stokes Raman Scattering (CARS) sensor. The sensor 102 is configured to probe the molecular composition of a target UAS material 116 at a distance. The system controller 104, comprising a high-speed FPGA or specialized processor, analyzes the returned spectral data to identify the specific chemical bonds of the airframe, such as the $\pi-\pi^*$ transitions in the aromatic rings of epoxy resins commonly used in carbon-fiber-reinforced polymers (CFRP).
[0013]
The core of the neutralization mechanism relies on the multi-channel laser assembly comprising a first source 106 (Pump laser) and a second source 108 (Stokes laser). Unlike conventional laser weapons that burn through material via thermal deposition, the present invention utilizes the STIRAP protocol to achieve coherent population transfer. As illustrated in FIG. 4, the target molecules are moved from a stable bonding ground state $|1\rangle$ to a repulsive (anti-bonding) electronic state $|3\rangle$. When a significant population of electrons resides in state $|3\rangle$, the molecular bonds effectively vanish, causing the material to lose all structural tension and disintegrate into a fine powder or gas.
[0014]
A critical aspect of the invention is the "counter-intuitive" pulse sequence shown in FIG. 3. The controller 104 modulates the laser sources such that the Stokes beam 108 reaches the target material 116 shortly before the pump beam 106. The mixing angle $\theta$ of the transition is defined by:
$$\tan \theta(t) = \frac{\Omega_p(t)}{\Omega_s(t)}$$
where $\Omega_p$ and $\Omega_s$ are the Rabi frequencies of the pump and Stokes pulses, respectively. By maintaining an adiabatic evolution where $\theta$ changes from $0$ to $\pi/2$, the system maintains a "dark state" polariton:
$$|D(t)\rangle = \cos \theta(t) |1\rangle - \sin \theta(t) |3\rangle$$
This dark state bypasses the intermediate state $|2\rangle$, ensuring that no energy is lost to spontaneous emission or thermal vibration, which typically causes the inefficiency and collateral heat seen in prior art.
[0015]
To maintain beam integrity through atmospheric turbulence, the system includes a Spatial Light Modulator (SLM) 110. The SLM 110 is configured to impart a helical phase-front to the Stokes beam, creating an optical vortex (Laguerre-Gaussian mode). This vortex creates a low-density "dark core" which acts as a guide for the pump beam, significantly reducing atmospheric divergence. Furthermore, the second source 108 is configured to generate a femtosecond filamentation pulse. When the peak power $P$ exceeds the critical power $P_{cr}$ (approximately 3-10 GW in air), self-focusing creates a plasma-mediated waveguide 114 that allows the STIRAP pulses to reach the target with diffraction-limited precision over long distances.

CLAIMS

  1. 1. A system for the non-kinetic dissociation of a target unmanned aerial system (UAS) comprising:
    a spectrographic sensor configured to detect a molecular-bond specific resonance in a target material of the UAS;
    a multi-channel laser assembly comprising at least a first source for generating a pump beam and a second source for generating a Stokes beam; and
    a controller operatively coupled to the sensor and the laser assembly, the controller configured to:
    determine a target electronic transition state for a molecular bonding agent in the target material based on data from the spectrographic sensor;
    execute a Stimulated Raman Adiabatic Passage (STIRAP) protocol by modulating the first and second sources to generate a counter-intuitive pulse sequence wherein the Stokes beam precedes the pump beam in time but overlaps spatially at the target; and
    adjust the spatiotemporal overlap of the Stokes and pump beams to maintain a dark-state polariton within the molecular bonding agent, thereby inducing a coherent population transfer from a bonding ground state to a repulsive electronic state.
  2. 2. The system of claim 1, wherein the controller is further configured to utilize the second source to generate a secondary filamentation pulse at a peak power exceeding the critical power for self-focusing in the atmosphere, thereby creating a transient plasma-mediated waveguide configured to channel the Stokes and pump beams to a sub-surface interface of the target material.
  3. 3. The system of claim 1, wherein the target material is a carbon-fiber-reinforced polymer (CFRP) and the controller is configured to tune the frequency of the pump beam to a transition energy specifically corresponding to a $\pi-\pi^*$ transition of aromatic rings in a resin matrix of said CFRP.
  4. 4. The system of claim 1, wherein the laser assembly further comprises a spatial light modulator (SLM) configured to impart a helical phase-front to the Stokes beam to create an optical vortex, wherein said optical vortex is configured to confine the pump beam within a dark core of the vortex to minimize atmospheric scattering.
  5. 5. The system of claim 1, wherein the spectrographic sensor is a coherent anti-Stokes Raman scattering (CARS) sensor configured to provide real-time feedback on a population density of the repulsive electronic state to the controller.
  6. 6. The system of claim 1, wherein the first source and the second source are tunable lasers selected from the group consisting of optical parametric oscillators, dye lasers, and solid-state lasers.
  7. 7. The system of claim 1, wherein the controller is configured to adjust the pulse sequence to ensure adiabatic evolution of the dark-state polariton such that $\Omega_s(t)$ dominates at a beginning of an interaction and $\Omega_p(t)$ dominates at a conclusion of the interaction.
  8. 8. The system of claim 1, wherein the repulsive electronic state is a non-bonding molecular orbital state that induces spontaneous mechanical failure of the target material without significant thermal deposition.
