Non-linear Adaptive Biasing Controller for Sub-threshold CMOS Logic Circuits

ABSTRACT

[0001] A system and method for modulating gate-bias currents in sub-threshold Complementary Metal-Oxide-Semiconductor (CMOS) logic. The system includes a differential process-monitoring circuit for detecting parasitic-to-intrinsic capacitive ratios, a transition-density estimator for monitoring switching activity, and an adaptive non-linear controller. The controller defines a dynamic sliding surface \( S \) based on switching activity and delay differentials, utilizing a discontinuous switching control law to generate a modulated gate-bias current. By dynamically adjusting gain coefficients, the controller suppresses chattering-induced power dissipation and decouples parasitic capacitance compensation from global process-voltage-temperature drift.

FIELD OF THE INVENTION

[0002] The present invention relates generally to semiconductor integrated circuits, and more particularly to bias control systems for digital logic operating in the sub-threshold (weak inversion) regime.

BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

[0003] Sub-threshold CMOS logic offers significant power savings by operating with a supply voltage lower than the threshold voltage (\( V_{th} \)) of the transistors. However, sub-threshold circuits are extremely sensitive to process variations, temperature fluctuations, and parasitic capacitance. In conventional sub-threshold designs, fixed biasing often fails to account for localized parasitic fluctuations or dynamic switching workloads, leading to timing violations or excessive leakage power.

[0004] There exists a need for a biasing controller that can provide non-linear, adaptive compensation for parasitic loads while maintaining stability across various switching activity levels without inducing high-frequency chattering in the power distribution network.

SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

[0005] To address the aforementioned needs, the present invention provides a non-linear sub-threshold biasing controller comprising a Parasitic Sensing Unit (PSU), a Transition-Density Estimator, and a Non-linear Bias Mapping Engine.

[0006] In one embodiment, the controller utilizes a sliding-mode control architecture to define a dynamic sliding surface. The control law dynamically modulates gain coefficients \( \alpha \) and \( \beta \) as a function of the instantaneous switching activity \( \lambda \), ensuring that the bias current \( I_{bias} \) accurately compensates for parasitic capacitance \( C_{para} \) without thermal runaway.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

[0010] Referring to FIG. 1, the system comprises a logic array 100 operating in the sub-threshold regime, monitored by a Transition-Density Estimator 110 and a Parasitic Sensing Unit (PSU) 120. The implementation is optimized for CMOS process nodes ranging from 180nm to 28nm FD-SOI.

[0011] The PSU 120 comprises a current-starved ring oscillator (CSRO) that mimics the load profile of the target sub-threshold logic. A dummy load capacitor, sized to approximately 110% of the estimated interconnect capacitance, serves as a reference. The CSRO frequency is converted to a proportional voltage \( V_{sense} \) via a frequency-to-voltage converter (FVC) consisting of a switched-capacitor integrator.

[0012] The Non-linear Controller 130 implements an adaptive sliding-mode control law. The mathematical definition of the dynamic sliding surface \( S \) is:

\[ S = \alpha(\lambda) \cdot (\Delta\tau - \Delta\tau_{ref}) + \beta(\lambda) \cdot \frac{d(\Delta\tau - \Delta\tau_{ref})}{dt} \]

where \( \Delta\tau \) is the differential delay signal, \( \Delta\tau_{ref} \) is the target reference, and \( \alpha(\lambda), \beta(\lambda) \) are gain coefficients dynamically modulated as a function of the switching activity metric \( \lambda \).

[0013] The High-Gain Control Stage 140 generates the modulated gate-bias current \( I_{bias} \). Due to the exponential relationship between drain current and gate voltage in sub-threshold operation:

\[ I_{bias} \propto \exp\left(\frac{V_{sense}}{\eta V_T}\right) \]

The controller 130 utilizes a 6-bit binary-weighted current Digital-to-Analog Converter (DAC) to perform a Taylor-series approximation of this exponential function, providing a piecewise linear mapping that stabilizes the delay \( \tau \) despite variations in \( C_{para} \).

[0014] Physical integration utilizes a "Star-Distribution" topology (FIG. 3) to ensure uniform current delivery. The \( V_{bias} \) routing is shielded by grounded metal layers (e.g., Metal 2 and Metal 4) to prevent capacitive coupling. In a 28nm FD-SOI embodiment, High-Threshold Voltage (HVT) transistors are utilized for the logic array 100, while Standard-Threshold Voltage (SVT) transistors are used for the controller 130.

[0015] Calibration is performed via an on-chip Finite State Machine (FSM) and One-Time Programmable (OTP) memory. The PI control law for initialization is defined as:

\[ Bias_{Out}[n] = K_p \cdot e[n] + K_i \cdot \sum e[n] \]

where \( e[n] \) represents the deviation from the target switching frequency. The coefficients \( K_p \) and \( K_i \) are hard-coded into the OTP memory during post-fabrication testing.

CLAIMS

  1. A biasing controller for sub-threshold Complementary Metal-Oxide-Semiconductor (CMOS) logic, comprising:
    • a differential process-monitoring circuit (120) configured to output a differential delay signal (\( \Delta\tau \)) indicating a ratio of parasitic-to-intrinsic capacitive load;
    • a transition-density estimator (110) configured to monitor an instantaneous switching activity metric (\( \lambda \)) of a logic array (100); and
    • an adaptive non-linear controller (130) operatively coupled to the differential process-monitoring circuit and the transition-density estimator, the adaptive non-linear controller comprising:
    • a switching manifold generator configured to define a dynamic sliding surface, S, according to the mathematical expression \( S = \alpha(\lambda) \cdot (\Delta\tau - \Delta\tau_{ref}) + \beta(\lambda) \cdot \frac{d(\Delta\tau - \Delta\tau_{ref})}{dt} \), wherein \( \Delta\tau_{ref} \) is a target reference, and \( \alpha(\lambda) \) and \( \beta(\lambda) \) are gain coefficients dynamically modulated as a function of the switching activity metric (\( \lambda \)); and
    • a high-gain control stage (140) configured to generate a modulated gate-bias current (\( I_{bias} \)) for the logic array according to a discontinuous switching control law defined by the sign of the dynamic sliding surface S.
  2. The biasing controller of claim 1, wherein the high-gain control stage (140) comprises a binary-weighted current Digital-to-Analog Converter (DAC) configured to implement a Taylor-series approximation of an exponential current-voltage relationship.
  3. The biasing controller of claim 1, wherein the differential process-monitoring circuit (120) comprises a current-starved ring oscillator (CSRO) and a frequency-to-voltage converter (FVC) utilizing a switched-capacitor integrator.
  4. The biasing controller of claim 1, further comprising a star-distribution routing network configured to deliver the gate-bias current to the logic array, wherein the routing network is shielded by at least two grounded metal layers.
  5. The biasing controller of claim 1, wherein the CMOS logic is fabricated on a 28nm Fully Depleted Silicon On Insulator (FD-SOI) process node utilizing High-Threshold Voltage (HVT) transistors for the logic array and Standard-Threshold Voltage (SVT) transistors for the adaptive non-linear controller.
  6. The biasing controller of claim 1, further comprising an on-chip One-Time Programmable (OTP) memory storing proportional-integral (PI) control coefficients \( K_p \) and \( K_i \) calibrated to compensate for die-to-die process variations.
  7. A method for controlling sub-threshold bias in a CMOS logic array, comprising:
    • sensing a differential delay (\( \Delta\tau \)) representing parasitic load;
    • estimating a switching activity metric (\( \lambda \));
    • calculating a dynamic sliding surface \( S \) as a function of \( \Delta\tau \) and \( \lambda \); and
    • modulating a gate-bias current \( I_{bias} \) based on a sign of the sliding surface \( S \) to maintain a constant delay-power product.
