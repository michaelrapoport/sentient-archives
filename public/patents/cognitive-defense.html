BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Inventor: Michael Rapoport from Polaritronics Inc.

FIELD OF THE INVENTION

[0004] The present invention relates generally to cognitive warfare defense systems, and more particularly to a system and method for adaptively disrupting attempts at neural entrainment or cognitive manipulation through the detection of endogenous rhythms and the generation of masking signals.

DESCRIPTION OF THE RELATED ART

[0005] Cognitive warfare involves manipulating an individual’s or group’s cognitive processes to influence their opinions, decisions, and behaviors. Neural entrainment, a key tactic in cognitive warfare, exploits the brain’s tendency to synchronize its neural oscillations with external stimuli. Existing countermeasures are often static and fail to adapt to the dynamic nature of cognitive attacks. These static defenses are easily bypassed or can even be exploited, causing further cognitive disruption.

[0006] Prior art approaches to mitigating neural entrainment have primarily focused on broad-spectrum noise generation or targeted stimulation based on pre-determined frequencies. However, these methods lack the adaptability required to effectively counter sophisticated cognitive attacks that continuously adjust their entrainment strategies. Furthermore, existing solutions often overlook the critical role of metabolic factors in modulating neural responses and the potential risks of exceeding cerebral oxygen extraction fraction (OEF) thresholds during intense stimulation.

[0007] Therefore, a need exists for a more adaptive and physiologically aware system that can dynamically detect and disrupt hostile neural entrainment attempts while minimizing the risk of adverse effects on the user's cognitive state and overall well-being.

BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

Inventor: Michael Rapoport from Polaritronics Inc.

[0008] The present invention addresses the shortcomings of the prior art by providing an adaptive cognitive defense system that utilizes endogenous rhythm detection to actively disrupt hostile attempts at neural entrainment or cognitive manipulation. The system comprises a neural sensor array, a metabolic sensor, and a processing unit configured to map neural oscillations into a high-dimensional manifold representing an endogenous attractor state. A Riemannian metric is calculated to define a local tangent space. An exogenous entrainment vector is identified, and a masking signal is generated within a null-space of the local tangent space. The masking signal is constrained to minimize geodesic deviation of the endogenous attractor state, modulated via a stochastic resonance protocol using 1/f noise, and dynamically scaled via a metabolic feedback loop coupled to the metabolic sensor. The invention maintains a cerebral oxygen extraction fraction (OEF) below a safety threshold. A countermeasure module injects the scaled masking signal. This adaptive approach allows for targeted disruption of entrainment attempts while preserving the integrity of the user's endogenous cognitive processes.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

Inventor: Michael Rapoport from Polaritronics Inc.

[0013] The present invention provides an adaptive cognitive defense system designed to actively disrupt attempts at neural entrainment or cognitive manipulation by utilizing endogenous rhythm detection. The system monitors and analyzes neural activity to identify exogenous entrainment vectors and generates masking signals to counter these attempts while preserving the integrity of the user's cognitive state.

[0014] The system comprises a neural sensor array, a metabolic sensor, a processing unit, and a countermeasure module. The neural sensor array is configured to capture neural oscillations from the user's brain. The array may include, but is not limited to, electroencephalography (EEG) electrodes, magnetoencephalography (MEG) sensors, or other suitable neural sensing technologies. The neural sensor array provides the input for the processing unit to map neural oscillations into a high-dimensional manifold.

[0015] The metabolic sensor is configured to measure cerebral oxygen extraction fraction (OEF) or other relevant metabolic parameters. This sensor provides a metabolic feedback loop that dynamically scales the amplitude of the masking signal to ensure the user's metabolic state remains within a safe threshold. Suitable metabolic sensors may include near-infrared spectroscopy (NIRS) devices or other non-invasive techniques for monitoring cerebral metabolism.

[0016] The processing unit is configured to perform several key functions: (a) mapping neural oscillations into a high-dimensional manifold representing an endogenous attractor state, (b) calculating a Riemannian metric of the high-dimensional manifold to define a local tangent space, (c) identifying an exogenous entrainment vector, (d) generating a masking signal within a null-space of the local tangent space, wherein the masking signal is constrained to minimize geodesic deviation of the endogenous attractor state by maintaining its topological invariants, (e) modulating the masking signal via a stochastic resonance protocol using 1/f noise, and (f) dynamically scaling the amplitude of the masking signal via a metabolic feedback loop coupled to the metabolic sensor, such that the scaling is a function of the local curvature of the high-dimensional manifold and maintains a cerebral oxygen extraction fraction (OEF) below a safety threshold.

[0017] The countermeasure module is configured to inject the scaled masking signal into the user's brain. In one embodiment, the countermeasure module comprises a high-density transcranial current stimulation (tDCS) array. The tDCS array is configured to adaptively steer current vectors into neural sub-regions identified as having the lowest topological stability margin relative to the identified exogenous entrainment vector. This allows for targeted disruption of entrainment attempts with minimal disruption to other cognitive processes.

[0018] The processing unit further maintains topological invariants by calculating the Lyapunov exponents of the endogenous attractor state and ensuring the masking signal does not induce a sign change in the maximum Lyapunov exponent. This prevents the system from destabilizing the user's cognitive state. Additionally, the processing unit employs a symplectic integrator to recompute the local tangent space and its corresponding null-space, thereby preserving the Hamiltonian energy-momentum of the endogenous attractor state during non-stationary drift. This ensures that the system remains stable and effective even when the user's cognitive state is changing rapidly.

[0019] The metabolic feedback loop scales the spectral density of the 1/f noise such that the masking signal power is maximized at manifold coordinates exhibiting the highest local curvature while remaining within the OEF safety threshold. This allows the system to dynamically adjust the masking signal to target the most vulnerable areas of the brain while ensuring the user's metabolic state remains within safe limits.
